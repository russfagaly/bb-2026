# 2026 Alameda Little League Majors — Pitching Performance Report
*Through games of April 8, 2026 · 76 game files · 52 pitchers with 1+ IP*

---

## Overview

Across the league this season, pitching has defined outcomes far more than batting. The teams winning consistently have leaned on two or three reliable arms who can eat innings without major damage — while teams struggling have often been undone by a handful of catastrophic outings from pitchers who either can't find the strike zone or get hit hard once hitters figure them out. This report breaks down performance across ERA, WHIP, strikeout rate, walk rate, hit allowance, strike percentage, and K/BB ratio, then synthesizes those categories to identify the true top performers.

For context, the minimum threshold used throughout is 6 innings pitched unless otherwise noted. Rates are calculated per 6 innings (K/6, BB/6, H/6) to match the typical 6-inning game format.

---

## ERA — Who Is Keeping Runs Off the Board

The most striking ERA story of the season belongs to **Lennon Danielsson (Guardians)**, who has posted a 0.93 ERA across 9.2 innings — the lowest mark among any pitcher with meaningful appearances. He has allowed just 1 earned run, struck out 15 batters, walked only 4, and thrown strikes 65.2% of the time. The sample is still growing, but the peripherals — K/BB of 3.75, solid hit suppression — suggest the number is real, not a fluke.

Right behind him is **Zeke Woo (Brewers)** with a 1.23 ERA in 14.2 innings, who tells a different but equally impressive story (more on him in the WHIP section). And then there is **Ace Beaver (White Sox)**, who has the most impressive ERA by context: 1.66 across 21.2 innings, the largest workload of any pitcher in the league. Maintaining a sub-2.00 ERA while absorbing that many innings is the clearest sign of a true top-of-the-rotation arm.

**Julian Paiso (Astros)** sits at 1.88 in 14.1 innings, the fourth-best ERA among regular starters, and does it without any obvious weakness in his profile. **Miles Denman (Padres)** at 1.80 in 9.2 innings rounds out the group of elite ERA pitchers, though his sample is the smallest of this tier.

Once you get past those five, there is a notable gap before **Cameron Le (Giants)** at 2.89 in 9.1 innings, and then **Roland Hatley (Yankees)** at 3.92 — which is respectable given that he's thrown 20.2 innings. No other pitcher with at least 12 innings thrown has an ERA under 4.00.

## WHIP — Controlling the Baserunners

ERA tells you how many runs score. WHIP tells you how often a pitcher is putting himself in trouble. The two don't always match.

**Zeke Woo (Brewers)** owns the most extraordinary WHIP in the league: **0.95** in 14.2 innings. He has allowed just 9 hits and 5 walks across those innings — meaning opponents are barely getting on base against him. His ERA of 1.23 is exactly where you'd expect it given that profile. Zeke is not a strikeout pitcher (14 total, 5.73 per 6 innings), which means he relies on defense and contact management, but he's doing it at an elite level.

**Adrien Romero (White Sox)** posts the second-best WHIP at **1.08** in 16.2 innings — elite by any standard. His 11 hits and 7 walks allowed in 16.2 innings is an extraordinary contact-suppression number. This makes his 4.86 ERA a genuine puzzle: how do you walk almost nobody, allow barely any hits, and still give up 9 earned runs? The answer appears to be timing — Adrien likely allows his baserunners to cluster, giving up multi-run innings even though his overall baserunner rate is excellent. His K/BB of 4.43 and 42.5% strikeout rate suggest he's dominant, and the ERA likely doesn't fully represent the threat he poses to hitters.

**Jordan Cerda-Zein (Astros)**, with a 1.36 WHIP in 10.2 innings, is third, though his ERA (5.73) also shows some disconnect — he almost never walks anyone (1.9% BB-rate, just 1 walk in 10.2 innings, K/BB of 18.0) but allows hits at a moderate rate. More on Jordan in the command section.

**Julian Paiso (Astros)** at 1.47 and **Roland Hatley (Yankees)** at 1.45 are strong, and **Ace Beaver (White Sox)** at 1.52 rounds out the tier of pitchers with both a strong WHIP and a meaningful innings total.

## Strikeouts — The Swing-and-Miss Crowd

Pure strikeout rate (K/6) can be misleading without context, because some of the league's highest strikeout pitchers are also getting hit hard. But it's still worth understanding who is making hitters miss.

**Adrien Romero (White Sox)** has the highest K-rate among high-volume pitchers: **11.16 K/6** and a league-leading **42.5% K%** among pitchers with 10+ innings. He is legitimately unhittable when he's on. **Ace Beaver (White Sox)** follows at 10.52 K/6 and 38.0% K%, and also leads the entire league in raw strikeout total with **38 strikeouts** — nearly 50% more than anyone with fewer innings.

**Kaleo Pontemayor (Marlins)** and **Jonah Lee (Giants)** both show dazzling K-rates (11.54 and 11.83 K/6 respectively), but each carries a significant caveat: Kaleo has a 13.15 ERA and Jonah has a 9.26 ERA. Both strike hitters out at impressive rates but get lit up for hits in between. Kaleo's H/6 of 11.54 and Jonah's H/6 of 15.43 — the worst in the league among regular pitchers — are the culprits.

**Huston Garvine (Brewers)**, **Zach Cheng (Brewers)**, and **Soren Krajewski (Guardians)** all cluster around 10–11 K/6, making the Brewers and Guardians two of the deeper strikeout-generating staffs in the league. Soren in particular shows a well-rounded profile (10.0 K/6, 34.2% K%, 6.60 ERA) though his walk rate is a concern.

**Wilder Eula (Marlins)** (9.82 K/6, 33.3% K%) and **Roland Hatley (Yankees)** (9.0 K/6, 31.6% K%) are notable for combining solid strikeout production with meaningful inning totals.

## Walk Control — The Command Artists

Walk rate may be the most separating stat at the youth level, and the spread in the league is enormous.

**Jordan Cerda-Zein (Astros)** is in a class by himself: **1 walk in 10.2 innings**, a BB% of **1.9%**, and a K/BB ratio of **18.0**. Those are numbers you simply don't see. He finds the strike zone at a rate (69.4% strikes — the highest in the league among significant arms) that borders on remarkable. His weakness is hit prevention — batters make contact when they swing — but his command is unmatched.

**Kaleo Pontemayor (Marlins)** and **Jonah Lee (Giants)** form an unusual pair: both have exceptional walk rates (Kaleo 4.6% BB, Jonah 4.2% BB) and excellent K/BB ratios (8.33 and 7.67 respectively), and both strike out lots of hitters, but both are getting crushed by batted balls. They are command pitchers who simply can't stop hits from landing.

**Zeke Woo (Brewers)** (7.8% BB) and **Adrien Romero (White Sox)** (9.6% BB) represent the elite of the pitchers who both command the zone and have the results to show for it. **Roland Hatley (Yankees)** (9.2% BB) and **Lennon Danielsson (Guardians)** (8.3% BB) are also in strong territory.

At the other end of the spectrum, **Soren Krajewski (Guardians)** walks 17.8% of batters he faces — the highest rate among pitchers with 12+ innings. **Huston Garvine (Brewers)** (18.8% BB) and **Zach Cheng (Brewers)** (20.0% BB) are two of the highest-walk pitchers in the league with real workloads. Their strikeout rates help them survive, but they consistently put themselves in jeopardy.

## Hit Suppression — Keeping the Ball in the Park

Some pitchers miss bats; others let batters make contact but keep it in control. H/6 measures how freely hits come.

**Zeke Woo (Brewers)** leads this category decisively at **3.68 H/6** — batters are simply not squaring him up. **Adrien Romero (White Sox)** follows at **3.96**, and **Cameron Le (Giants)** at **3.86 H/6** in 9.1 innings is impressive. **Julian Paiso (Astros)** at 4.60 H/6 is excellent, as is **Soren Krajewski (Guardians)** at 5.20 H/6 — his walk rate is the concern, not his ability to limit contact quality.

**Ace Beaver (White Sox)** (5.82 H/6) and **Roland Hatley (Yankees)** (6.1 H/6) are in the respectable middle tier — not dominant, but controlled.

The struggles come with **Jonah Lee (Giants)** at 15.43 H/6 — opponents are getting two and a half hits per inning against him, which is why his ERA is so high despite his command and strikeout rate. **Kaleo Pontemayor (Marlins)** (11.54 H/6) has the same issue in a less extreme form. **Drew Bryant (Guardians)** at 15.3 H/6 and **Jaxon Mooningham (Giants)** at 15.1 H/6 are also taking significant damage.

## Strike Percentage — Finding the Zone

Strike percentage matters because it underlies everything: more strikes means more hitters in difficult counts, fewer free passes, and less pitcher fatigue.

**Jordan Cerda-Zein (Astros)** leads meaningful arms at **69.4%**, with **Roland Hatley (Yankees)** second among high-volume pitchers at **65.9%**. **Lennon Danielsson (Guardians)** at **65.2%** and **Kaleo Pontemayor (Marlins)** at **67.9%** are also elite.

**Jonah Lee (Giants)** at 67.8% throws an enormous number of strikes — and still gets hit. This is one of the more puzzling profiles in the league: fantastic command, high strikeout rate, but hitters are making hard contact when they do connect.

**RJ Fagaly (Padres)** at 61.8% and **Julian Paiso (Astros)** at 61.7% are solid. Several pitchers cluster in the 59–61% range, which is functional but leaves room for improvement.

The command concerns are clearest with **Soren Krajewski** (55.6%), **Zach Cheng** (55.3%), **Miles Denman** (54.7%), and **Henry Schafer (Yankees)** (53.3%).

## Blended Analysis — The True Top Performers

Looking across all categories together, a clear picture emerges of who is genuinely pitching at an elite level this season.

### Tier 1: The Complete Package

**Ace Beaver (White Sox)** is the most complete pitcher in the league when you factor in workload. He leads the league in innings (21.2), strikeouts (38), and ranks near the top in ERA (1.66), K/6 (10.52), K% (38.0%), and WHIP (1.52). His 60.6% strike rate is functional rather than elite, and his walk rate of 12.0% is the only soft spot. But there is no other pitcher in the league who has done what he's done for this long at this level. He is the clearest top-of-rotation arm in the league.

**Zeke Woo (Brewers)** is the most statistically pure pitcher in the league: best WHIP (0.95), best hit suppression (3.68 H/6), second-lowest ERA among starters (1.23), and excellent walk control (7.8% BB). The only knock is strikeout rate — at 5.73 K/6, he is a contact manager, not a swing-and-miss arm. If opponents start squaring him up more frequently, the ERA could climb. But through this point in the season, he has been as unhittable as anyone.

**Lennon Danielsson (Guardians)** has the lowest ERA in the league (0.93) in a growing sample (9.2 IP), backed by a 3.75 K/BB, 65.2% strike rate, and 15 strikeouts. He's someone to watch closely.

### Tier 2: Excellent with a Caveat

**Julian Paiso (Astros)** (1.88 ERA, 1.47 WHIP, 8.79 K/6, 61.7% Strike%) is a well-rounded No. 2 starter type. The only soft spot is his walk rate (BB/6 of 4.19, BB% of 15.9%), which means he occasionally gets himself into trouble. But when he's commanding well, he's nearly unhittable.

**Adrien Romero (White Sox)** (42.5% K%, 1.08 WHIP, 11.16 K/6) has the most dominant raw stuff of any high-volume pitcher in the league. His ERA (4.86) doesn't match his peripherals, suggesting results are running against him — he may be the most likely candidate for ERA regression in the right direction as the season continues. The stuff is elite.

**Roland Hatley (Yankees)** (20.2 IP, 1.45 WHIP, 65.9% Strike%, 9.0 K/6, 3.44 K/BB) earns credit for being one of the most reliable workhorses in the league. His 3.92 ERA is respectable for the innings total, and his profile — great command, solid strikeout rate, strong K/BB — is consistent. He won't overwhelm you statistically, but he delivers inning after inning.

**Jordan Cerda-Zein (Astros)** is in a category of his own for command: 69.4% strikes, K/BB of 18.0, BB% of 1.9%. His ERA (5.73) is the outlier in an otherwise elite profile, driven by a moderate hit rate (7.64 H/6). He may simply be at the age/level where his velocity or stuff isn't quite enough to finish hitters off despite pinpoint control. If that changes, he becomes a nightmare.

### Tier 3: Solid Contributors

**Miles Denman (Padres)** (1.80 ERA, 9.3 IP), **Cameron Le (Giants)** (2.89 ERA, 9.1 IP), and **RJ Fagaly (Padres)** (4.50 ERA, 20.0 IP, 61.8% Strike%) each contribute meaningfully. Denman and Le show strong results in shorter samples. Fagaly's volume (20.0 IP, 30 strikeouts) is impressive even if the ERA has some room to improve.

**Wilder Eula (Marlins)** (6.75 ERA, 14.2 IP) has legitimate swing-and-miss stuff (9.82 K/6, 33.3% K%) and reasonable command, but hasn't been able to keep the ERA down. If the hit rate (6.95 H/6) dips slightly, the ERA could improve considerably.

### The Puzzling Profiles

Several pitchers present genuine paradoxes that are worth monitoring:

**Jonah Lee (Giants)** throws strikes constantly (67.8%), walks almost no one (4.2% BB), and strikes out hitters at an impressive rate (11.83 K/6) — yet carries a 9.26 ERA driven by an unsustainable 15.43 H/6 hit rate. Opponents are making hard contact when they connect. Whether this is a velocity/stuff issue, a pitch mix issue, or statistical noise, it bears watching.

**Kaleo Pontemayor (Marlins)** similarly strikes out 38.5% of batters and walks only 4.6% — an elite K/BB of 8.33 — while carrying a 13.15 ERA and 11.54 H/6. The pattern of "great command, great strikeouts, terrible hit prevention" is unusual and may indicate that his go-to pitch is hittable when batters do make contact.

**Adrien Romero**, as noted above, is the inverse puzzle: elite peripheral stats, ERA that looks worse than it should. The season will reveal which number is more real.

## Staff-Level Observations

**White Sox** have the deepest one-two punch in the league: Ace Beaver at the top of every volume-and-effectiveness category, and Adrien Romero with the most dominant raw stuff. If Adrien's ERA normalizes downward, this is the most feared rotation in the league.

**Brewers** have a high-ceiling staff with Zeke Woo's elite prevention numbers and Huston Garvine's strikeout ability, but walk rate remains a chronic problem for multiple arms. Zach Cheng (20.0% BB) and Huston Garvine (18.8% BB) combined throw over 25 innings of high-walk pitching.

**Guardians** have a strong foundation in Lennon Danielsson and a solid depth piece in Soren Krajewski, but early results from Conor Fogarty (7.04 ERA, 15.1 IP) and Drew Bryant (22.95 ERA, 6.2 IP) have dragged the staff's overall ERA.

**Astros** have remarkable diversity: Jordan Cerda-Zein's command wizardry, Julian Paiso's balanced excellence, and a collection of other arms at varying effectiveness.

**Yankees** lean heavily on Roland Hatley's workload (20.2 IP) as the anchor. Hudson Patterson has shown flashes but his 6.55 ERA in 10.2 innings needs improvement.

**Padres** have RJ Fagaly doing yeoman's work as the innings leader alongside Miles Denman's impressive early numbers, but Samuel Gilmore (5.94 ERA, 16.2 IP) has been a bit inconsistent.

**Marlins** and **Giants** have struggled as staffs, with multiple pitchers posting ERAs above 7.00. Julian Warner (Marlins, 5.30 ERA) and Wilder Eula are the steadiest arms for those clubs.
